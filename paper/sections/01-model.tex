\section{Model, statement, and proof architecture}\label{sec:model}
Let $\Lambda_{n,m}=(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)\times(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)$.
Throughout the physical argument, $n,m$ are even and at least four. Let
$\mathcal E_{n,m}$ contain the two positive-direction edges from each vertex;
these are precisely the undirected nearest-neighbor edges counted once.
There are $A=nm$ rungs, $2A$ spins, $2A$ edges, and Hilbert-space dimension
$4^A$. Put $S^\alpha=\sigma^\alpha/2$ for the usual Pauli matrices. We study
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
 H_{n,m}(g)=\sum_{r\in\Lambda_{n,m}}
 \left(\boldsymbol S_{r,1}\cdot\boldsymbol S_{r,2}+\frac34\id\right)
 +g\sum_{\{r,s\}\in\mathcal E_{n,m}}\sum_{a=1}^2
       \boldsymbol S_{r,a}\cdot\boldsymbol S_{s,a}.
\end{equation}
The rung coupling is one. The additive constant changes no spectral difference.
Write the eigenvalues, \emph{including multiplicities}, as
$E_0\le E_1\le\cdots\le E_{4^A-1}$. Write $Z_{n,m}(\beta)=\tr e^{-\beta H_{n,m}}$
and $Q_{n,m}(\beta)=e^{\beta E_0}Z_{n,m}(\beta)$.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform finite-volume gap]\label{thm:main}
For every real $g\in[0,1/10]$ and every even integer $L\ge256$, the Hamiltonian
\eqref{eq:H} on $\Lambda_{L,L}$ has a simple lowest eigenvalue, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 E_j-E_0>\delta_*:=\frac{\log(200/13)}{1024}
 \quad(1\le j<4^{L^2}).
\end{equation}
Numerically, $\delta_*\approx0.002669304696$; the logarithmic expression, not
this decimal, is the proved constant.
\end{theorem}

The formal endpoint is
\lean{BilayerDressed.CubicPhysicalGap.standard_spin_uniform_gap}.
It constructs a complete invertible complex eigenbasis for the explicit Pauli
matrix and proves uniqueness and separation with multiplicities. There are no
seed, table, residual, transfer-convergence, or ground-simplicity hypotheses
remaining in this endpoint.

The proof has two independently intelligible parts. Sections~\ref{sec:rung}--\ref{sec:seed}
construct and certify three finite physical partition-function ratios. Sections~\ref{sec:transfer}--\ref{sec:allsize}
prove the dimension-independent propagation principle and apply it to those
ratios. No large reference space is diagonalized numerically. Its role is an
explicit finite-dimensional comparison in a proof.

\paragraph{Context and scope.}
Weak-coupling stability for product-state lattice systems is classical; see
Yarotsky's Theorem~1~\cite{yarotsky} and the higher-dimensional block-diagonalization
result, Theorem~5.3, of Del Vecchio--Fr\"ohlich--Pizzo--Rossi~\cite{delvecchio}.
These results give important structural context, not the particular rational
threshold certified here. The spatial-moment and coupled-purity strategy is
motivated mathematically by the spin-one Haldane-gap manuscript~\cite{haldane},
notably Proposition~3.1, Lemmas~4.1--4.2, Proposition~4.3, and its finite
initializations in Section~5. The present proof constructs its own two-dimensional
transfer approximants and all its model-specific finite inputs. It does not
import the Haldane theorem as an axiom or reuse its numerical seed.
The contribution established here is the displayed explicit interval and
end-to-end finite certificate. We do not infer from this that no stronger
published quantitative bound exists, nor that the physically expected entire
dimer phase has been covered. Earlier narrower certificates for this same
bilayer are proof-development comparisons, not a second physical model.
